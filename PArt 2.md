1. Housing Maintenance Code Complaints and Problems
https://data.cityofnewyork.us/Housing-Development/Housing-Maintenance-Code-Complaints-and-Problems/ygpa-z7cr/about_data

Our group is interested in this dataset because it documents the housing problems residents report. We will use it to investigate how concerns vary across neighborhoods and seasons. We could examine which problems are most common, whether certain buildings receive repeated complaints, and how reporting patterns differ across boroughs. For a predictive project, we could investigate whether a building’s past complaints help predict its number of reported problems in a later period.

2. Housing Maintenance Code Violations
https://data.cityofnewyork.us/Housing-Development/Housing-Maintenance-Code-Violations/wvxf-dwi5/about_data 

Our group is interested in this dataset because it records housing-code violations and their severity. We can use it to study documented housing conditions alongside residents’ complaints. We could investigate where hazardous violations are concentrated, which buildings experience recurring violations, and whether previous complaints predict future violations. Combining this dataset with complaint records would allow us to explore the relationship between reported problems and subsequent enforcement findings.

3. U.S. Department of Agriculture, Economic Research Service 
SNAP-authorized Retailer Access Map

https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/food-access-research-atlas/mapping-tools

Our group is interested in this dataset because low-income households may face transportation barriers when accessing stores that accept SNAP benefits. We could examine whether communities with higher poverty rates or more households without vehicles have poorer geographic access to SNAP-authorized retailers. Combining these measures with Census demographic data could help us predict which communities face the greatest access barriers and explore differences between urban and rural areas.
